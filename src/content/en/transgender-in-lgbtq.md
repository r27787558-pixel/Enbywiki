# The 'T' in LGBTQ+

The "T" in LGBTQ+ is both central and contested. Transgender people helped build the modern movement, yet they have repeatedly been asked to wait, to soften themselves, or to leave. This page traces that relationship.

## How the acronym grew

The names communities use for themselves record their politics:

- **1950s–60s** – "homophile" organisations, cautious and assimilationist.
- **1970s** – "gay liberation," then "gay and lesbian."
- **1980s** – "lesbian and gay," as women's leadership grew.
- **1990s** – **LGBT** becomes standard, as bisexuals and transgender people are written into the name.
- **2010s–present** – **LGBTQIA+** and similar forms add queer, intersex, asexual and more.

The "T" was not handed to trans people. It was pushed for by trans activists — including veterans of STAR and groups like Transsexual Menace — who argued that a movement for sexual freedom could not ignore gender.

## Shared ground

Trans and cis LGBQ+ people face overlapping harms:

- The same **police violence** and the same **anti-sodomy laws** that once criminalised gay people were used against gender variance.
- The same **medical establishment** pathologised both same-sex desire and trans identity.
- The same **hostility from families, schools and employers** drives homelessness and poor mental health in both communities.

That shared experience is real. In surveys, trans people are among the most likely of all LGBTQ+ groups to report discrimination and violence — which is one reason solidarity matters.

## Fault lines

Yet the alliance has never been smooth:

| Tension | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **Exclusion from events** | Sylvia Rivera was jeered off stage at the 1973 Pride rally |
| **Feminist separatism** | The Michigan Womyn's Music Festival excluded trans women for decades, until 2015 |
| **"Drop the T"** | 2010s slogans urging the LGBTQ+ movement to abandon trans people |
| **Medical gatekeeping** | Trans people must often secure approval that gay and lesbian people no longer need |
| **Media representation** | Trans stories are often told by cis people, or as tragedy rather than life |

None of these are ancient history. They happen now.

## Why the "T" stays

The case for trans inclusion is both practical and moral:

- Trans people are not a special interest within LGBTQ+; they are part of its foundation. The movement's founding riots were led in part by trans women.
- Attacks on trans people are used to justify attacks on everyone who fails to conform. Anti-trans laws normalize surveillance of bodies, dress and speech for all of us.
- A movement that abandons its most vulnerable members is not liberatory. It is just power with new rules.

## Belonging

The "T" does not sit at the edge of the acronym. It sits at the heart of the question the whole movement asks: what right do other people have to decide who we are?

Understanding that question means understanding trans history — the millennia of gender diversity in [Ancient Origins](/docs/ancient-world), the colonial erasure, the clinics and the riots, and the people still fighting. The story of the "T" is not a footnote to LGBTQ+ history. It is one of its main chapters.
