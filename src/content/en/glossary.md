# Glossary of Terms

Language about gender changes quickly, and different communities use the same word in different ways. These are working definitions, written in plain language — not the last word.

## Core terms

| Term | Working definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Sex assigned at birth** | The label (usually "male" or "female") given to a baby based on the appearance of its body, without regard to how it will later identify. |
| **Gender identity** | A person's internal sense of their own gender. It cannot be observed from the outside. |
| **Gender expression** | How a person presents gender to others — clothing, voice, hair, name, pronouns, mannerisms. |
| **Transgender (trans)** | An umbrella term for people whose gender identity differs from the sex they were assigned at birth. |
| **Cisgender** | A person whose gender identity matches the sex they were assigned at birth. |
| **Non-binary** | A person whose gender is not exclusively "man" or "woman." Some non-binary people also identify as trans. |
| **Genderqueer** | A related umbrella term for genders outside the binary, often with a political or fluid emphasis. |
| **Agender** | Having no gender, or a very weak or absent sense of gender. |
| **Genderfluid** | A gender identity that shifts over time. |

## Transition and care

| Term | Working definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Transition** | The process of living in alignment with one's gender. It may be social, legal, and/or medical, and every person's path differs. |
| **Gender-affirming care** | Medical care that supports a person's gender, such as hormone therapy or surgery. Sometimes called gender-confirming care. |
| **Hormone replacement therapy (HRT)** | The use of hormones to change secondary sex characteristics. |
| **Gender dysphoria** | Distress caused by the mismatch between a person's gender and their assigned sex or body. Not every trans person experiences it. |
| **Gender euphoria** | The joy or relief that comes from being recognised in one's gender. |
| **Detransition** | Stopping or reversing a transition. It happens for many reasons, most commonly pressure, cost, or loss of support — not usually because the person was not trans. |

## Identity, culture and respect

| Term | Working definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Deadname** | The name a trans person was given at birth but no longer uses. Using it without permission is called deadnaming. |
| **Misgender** | To refer to someone with a gender they do not hold, e.g. the wrong pronouns. |
| **Passing** | Being read by others as the gender one lives as. A useful but contested idea, because it can imply that being recognised is the goal. |
| **Outing** | Revealing a person's trans status without their consent. |
| **T4T** | "Trans for trans" — trans people forming relationships with other trans people. |
| **Two-Spirit** | An English umbrella term adopted by Indigenous North Americans in 1990 for culturally specific gender roles. It belongs to Indigenous communities and is not a general synonym for "transgender" or "gay." |
| **Hijra** | A recognised third-gender community in South Asia, with a long history and its own social structures. |
| **Intersex** | Born with bodies that do not fit typical definitions of male or female. Intersex is about biology, and being intersex is distinct from being transgender — though some people are both. |
| **Cross-dresser** | A person who wears clothing associated with a different gender. It is not the same as being transgender and is often about expression rather than identity. |
| **Drag** | A performance art centred on exaggerated or theatrical gender presentation. Drag performers may be of any gender identity. |

## Why words matter

Terms like *transsexual* and *transvestite* appear throughout historical medical writing. They are not slurs, but they are dated and clinical, and many trans people today do not use them for themselves. Where this wiki uses them, it is usually to describe how past generations named their experiences.

Language will keep changing. What remains constant is the principle behind it: people get to name themselves.
