# Key Figures

Transgender history is made of people. Some are famous; most are ordinary. This page gathers short profiles of figures who shaped the story — scholars, activists, artists, and martyrs. It is necessarily incomplete.

## A few in more depth

### Magnus Hirschfeld

Hirschfeld was a physician who believed science could liberate. He founded the world's first gay-rights organisation, coined "transvestite" as a clinical term, and built an institute that offered real care to real people. The Nazis burned it in 1933. His career shows both the promise and the limits of asking medicine to grant dignity.

### Dora Richter

For decades, Richter was a footnote — a housemaid at Hirschfeld's institute who became, in 1931, the first known person to undergo vaginoplasty. Her disappearance after the Nazi raid is a reminder of how many trans lives were erased not by chance but by violence.

### Marsha P. Johnson and Sylvia Rivera

Two trans women of colour, veterans of the Stonewall uprising, founded STAR and STAR House to shelter homeless trans youth. Rivera's 1973 speech "Y'all Better Quiet Down" remains one of the great documents of trans rage and dignity. Johnson died in 1992; her death was initially ruled a suicide, and the case was later reopened.

### Christine Jorgensen

Her 1952 transition was front-page news worldwide. Rather than retreat, Jorgensen worked as an actress and lecturer, using her platform to argue for trans people's ordinariness and right to privacy. She was among the first to carry the community's story to a mass audience.

### Monica Helms

A US Navy veteran, Helms designed the Transgender Pride Flag in 1999 as a symbol that would always be "correct," however it was flown. Its light blue, pink and white stripes now appear at marches on every continent.

### Leslie Feinberg

Feinberg's novel *Stone Butch Blues* (1993) and history *Transgender Warriors* (1996) gave a generation both a story and a lineage. Ze insisted that trans liberation was inseparable from class struggle and from the rights of all oppressed people.
